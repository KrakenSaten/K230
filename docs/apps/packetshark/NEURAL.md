# PacketShark NEURAL

**Design study for owner review · based on master `448327d` · 24 November 2026**

**Recommendation — Incident Timeline.** Give the person holding DOORS a way to answer “what changed around the moment I noticed the problem?” They can mark a symptom, compare the traffic before and after it, and open the observations behind a possible explanation. This works with the capture engine already on master. The first research decision is whether a person can distinguish a service problem from a gap in what the unit saw.

## Three products that could grow from the same capture

| Concept | What the operator does | Organizing data structure | Where it earns its place |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Incident Timeline** | Tap **Mark symptom** during a stall. Select the mark later and walk from a change in behavior to the packets that support it. | An ordered, bounded journal of episodes, user marks and observation gaps. A before/after query returns evidence and competing explanations. | Intermittent faults on the unit’s own connection. Valuable in one short session. |
| **Traffic Atlas** | Select an observed participant. Turn from “who spoke with whom?” to “what did this conversation resemble?” | A bounded, directed conversation matrix. Each edge holds a compact rhythm of packet direction, size and intervals. Compare edges within this capture. | Comparing visible exchanges even under encryption. Most valuable when the unit itself participates in several flows. |
| **Network Doctor** | Choose a symptom such as “names resolve slowly.” Watch the instrument decide which part of that question this interface can answer. | A small graph of diagnostic questions. Each branch specifies required observations, counterevidence and when to return “insufficient evidence.” | Guided troubleshooting for occasional operators. It can explain which evidence to wait for before another capture would help. |

**Incident Timeline** treats the operator’s mark as evidence of *when a symptom was felt*. It survives packet-ring turnover as a timestamp in session memory. Coalesce repeated events into an episode with a count and first/last evidence references. Smart Capture would suggest a moment to inspect while the person is present. It would consume neither a user pin nor a byte of permanent storage automatically. A rolling, within-session reference makes a Time Machine view possible even on the first visit. A truncated window or expired reference remains visible as such.

**Traffic Atlas** puts one participant and its strongest observed relationships on the handheld. A tap on an edge opens a small direction-and-time plot and supporting rows. DNS, DHCP and ARP supply observed roles. Traffic DNA could group *similar shapes* across flows, with “unclassified” for short or ambiguous samples. Periodic traffic earns a period and a jitter range. Ordinary keepalives are a control case for beacon detection. A shared CDN, NAT, address rotation or one-way visibility defeats many application and device labels. Keep identity session-local and topology limited to observed relationships. The principal cost is that a convincing map can be drawn from a very partial sample.

**Network Doctor** starts with paired DNS timings, TCP handshakes and explicit error responses. It ranks explanations by which observation would discriminate between them. For example, repeated queries plus a visible SERVFAIL support a resolver-side response problem. Queries with no visible return and substantial shedding leave the question open. The next prompt can ask the operator to observe an ordinary retry. Its diagnostic reach grows one evidence-backed question at a time. It needs curated alternatives for each question, which makes it the most expensive concept to keep accurate. “Predictive health” belongs here only if a trend predicts a defined future symptom earlier than a simple threshold on held-out sessions.

Choose Timeline first because the same episode can help an expert inspect a fault and a novice explain what happened. Atlas is the better choice if field trials consistently reveal enough distinct conversations and operators ask about relationships. Doctor wins if guided questions improve correct diagnoses over inspecting the same evidence unaided. An offline, versioned threat-indicator match could eventually annotate a local episode with source, age and exact match criterion. Shared infrastructure and stale lists warrant an explicit alternative explanation. Defer that dependency until it answers a field question better than the existing evidence.

## One interaction, from symptom to packet

Illustrative portrait sheet reached from LIVE’s existing graph. Figures below are fictional.

```text
LIVE   ETH0   HEADERS              02:18
             [Mark symptom]

02:12   Symptom marked
        20 s before  |  20 s after · collecting

        DNS replies slowed
        Median 34 → 410 ms · 8 / 6 pairs
        Three TCP handshakes stayed near 40 ms

        [Examine DNS pairs]
```

The denominators are the matched observations in each window. Update the after-window count only as replies arrive. With one pair, display the pair and withhold the trend. A tap opens the matched queries and replies, then INSPECT or the surrounding LIST. The sheet should say “Other visible connections opened promptly. Resolver processing and the path to it are both possible.” A held interval gets a gap mark and a reason. Stale evidence reads “Packet expired” and keeps its original reference. Do not silently redirect the tap to a newer packet.

Use DS §59's protocol hues on protocol marks, its warning glyph and word for actionable findings, and text for certainty. Keep capture state, Companion readership and session controls visible. Portrait progressively reveals detail. Landscape keeps episode selection on the left and evidence on the right. At Large text, stack the evidence and scroll the body. Quiet sessions show the observed duration and coverage. The histogram stays useful with zero findings. ## Preliminary architecture

1. **Observe once in the helper.** Feed existing decoder, statistics, signals and network events into a bounded window accumulator. Start with eligible DNS pair counts and timings, TCP handshake counts, class totals and direction where available. Key each window by session and relative time. Attach interface, collection mode, drops, shed, HOLD, clock discontinuity, eviction and directional coverage. When a counter has only coarse timing, mark the overlapping window uncertain. No analysis may delay polling or consume ring capacity reserved for captured packets.
2. **Make observations reproducible.** A finding carries its rule or model version, interval, numerator and denominator, comparison population, supporting record IDs, contrary observations and reasons to abstain. Record the measurement independently of its proposed explanation. The deterministic path pairs transactions, estimates robust medians, checks periodicity and emits episodes. A model can propose a window for inspection. Publish a diagnostic claim only when a named comparison or protocol event supports the wording. Multiple scores derived from the same pairs count as one source of evidence.
3. **Bound cost and adaptation.** As a sizing hypothesis, 120 bins × 16 two-byte features take 3,840 bytes before masks, timestamps and indexes. Keep a fixed episode ring and cap tracked conversations independently. Account for all new allocations against the ADR’s 32 MiB packet-memory bound and measure total RSS separately. Freeze the reference during a candidate incident, HOLD or loss. Compare equal-duration windows on the same interface with sufficient eligible pairs. Expire both reference and identifiers at session end. On overload, skip optional work, count skips and keep the capture controls responsive.
4. **Give acceleration a narrow contract.** First compare median/MAD or change-point scoring on the CPU with a small temporal convolution over fixed bins and a compact embedding of direction/size sequences. A quantized temporal model could rank multivariate shifts. An embedding could retrieve similar Atlas conversations. Evaluate each against its own inexpensive baseline. Include missingness masks and quantization scales. Never turn endpoint names or addresses into model inputs. Probe supported tensor shapes, operator placement, copies and CPU fallback with the pinned toolchain. Reuse Vision’s host-fake pattern, model inspection and cleanup lessons. Avoid the camera, AI2D image preprocessing and detector weights.
5. **Keep inference disposable.** First bench fixed tensors offline. A live candidate would need a session-scoped, capability-free inference worker receiving only bounded numeric windows, with no capture descriptor, file access to captures or gateway endpoint. A single pending slot can replace stale work. Ignore late or mismatched session results. Retain CPU analysis when the worker is busy, missing or fails. Before introducing a worker, settle ownership with Vision, stop deadlines and abnormal-exit CMA recovery. Repeated forced kills can strand CMA even when the UI recovers. An unresolved recovery path keeps this experiment offline.
6. **Keep views small.** Extend the helper’s versioned line protocol only for bounded local summaries. The shell formats them and resolves evidence through its existing viewport. Admit a new detector when it declares required features, memory cap, minimum coverage, expiry and a reproducible fixture. Each contributes a finding to an existing view. The same contract can feed Atlas or Doctor later without adding tabs per detector.

**Retention and replay.** Baseline Compare initially uses two ranges of the current session. A later local file review could run the helper’s file source against an explicitly selected saved PCAP and drive the same timeline using recorded time. Saved captures are currently listed and deleted in the app. Reopening one would require review of file access, malformed input and lifecycle. A HEADERS save may omit aged-out bytes, earlier history and live quality counters. Show those as unknown on replay. Playback advances the analysis clock locally. There is no transmission path. Persistent learned profiles or automatic incident files would require a separate retention decision.

**Companion.** Its existing scoped statistics and finding totals can show *when to look at the unit*. The evidence drill-down lives on the panel. Host labels, timings per flow, embeddings, finding text, incident marks, rows and files stay local. Follow the existing physical confirmation for a requested START and the existing HOLD/STOP semantics. Review any new aggregate against the explicit field allowlist before proposing it.

## Experiments and handoff

These are proposed gates, not measurements. Run the first two before model selection. Use generated fixtures and consented data with documented provenance for any later evaluation. Split by site and session before windowing so adjacent windows or repeated devices cannot leak across train and evaluation sets.

| Order and open question | Smallest discriminating experiment | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Can we tell slow replies from missing visibility?** | Extend the existing synthetic DNS/TCP cases on paper with four matched traces: slow paired replies, one-sided replies, HOLD across recovery and loss during a healthy exchange. Specify expected counts, gap labels, evidence expiry and wording. Compare against M11/M12 findings. | Require every degraded case to abstain from causal attribution and every cited pair to resolve or explicitly expire. If timeline adds no discriminating evidence, improve current findings first. |
| **2. Does a mark help a person diagnose?** | Compare the existing SIGNALS workflow with the portrait and Large-text landscape mockups on the same shuffled cases. Record time to correct evidence, incorrect confident answers, taps and whether users notice a gap. | Advance if time to correct evidence improves without increasing confident errors. Test quiet captures as well as incidents. Select Doctor if novices consistently need a question before they can use a timeline. |
| **3. Is there a learnable residual?** | Compare rules against fixed-window anomaly and similarity candidates on held-out sites, including changed traffic mixes and benign periodic services. Measure false alerts per observed hour, precision at matched recall, lead time, abstention coverage and errors among accepted findings. Remove identifiers and shuffle timing as controls. | Pursue a model only for a reproducible gain at the same false-alert budget. Reject a classifier that mostly learns the site or loses its advantage under missing replies. |
| **4. Can KPU inference pay for itself?** | On a separately authorized bench, compare CPU and KPU on identical quantized windows. Measure preprocessing, queue/copy time, inference and result latency at p50/p95, RSS/CMA, energy per window, shell latency and drops against capture alone. Exercise warm/cold start, Vision contention, refusal and close. Use the outstanding Unit A line-rate gate before live adoption. | Keep CPU if the full pipeline fails to improve latency or energy at equivalent accuracy, increases capture loss, or leaves memory unrecovered outside measured idle noise. Hardware speed and capacity are open questions. |
