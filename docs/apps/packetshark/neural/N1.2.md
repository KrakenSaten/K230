## 2. The bench

### 2.1 Method (frozen before the scored run)

The method, the scenarios and the evaluation are in `research/packetshark-neural/bench/METHOD.md`. They were frozen at commit `262889f4`, before any scored session ran:

| File | SHA-256 prefix |
| --- | --- |
| `bench.py` | `64ff00635be8` |
| `bench_replay.c` | `d27085f0bc0a` |
| `scenarios.py` | `ae330718e898` |
| `evaluate.py` | `4eed1f0f26f9` |
| `run_all.py` | `c9de53582c1c` |

In short:

- **Isolation.** Linux network namespaces on the build host, joined by veth links to a bridge of their own. No interface of the host's network is used, and nothing reaches a LAN, Wi-Fi or the internet. Every endpoint is owned by the bench.
- **Real exchanges.** A DNS resolver, a DHCP server, six DHCP clients with back-off, four ARP peers on periodic cycles, bulk UDP, and the device's own DNS questions and a ping. All of them put real frames on the wire.
- **The real helper.** `pos-capture` built with libpcap from this branch, with F1's identity switch, network namespace and seccomp filter. It is driven over its socketpair as the shell drives it: `start dev0 headers 0 all 3600`, then HOLD, STOP and SAVE.
- **Artifacts made the way they happen:**
  - capture loss: the helper is stopped for 2.5–4 s during a blast, so the kernel ring overflows and the kernel's own drop counters report it;
  - HOLD: the helper's own `hold`;
  - incomplete visibility: the resolver's route back to the device is moved to a second link for 20–35 s.
- **Replay.** The saved capture and a sidecar of the helper's lines (state, lost-frame readings, shedding) go through the frozen engine with `bench_replay`.
- **Ground truth.** Taken only from the endpoints' own logs.
- **Data handling.** Raw captures and logs stay outside Git (`C:\K230-artifacts\n12-bench\run1.tgz`, 4.3 MB). They hold only bench addresses and synthetic names. The branch keeps the aggregate results (`research/packetshark-neural/bench/results/bench_results.json`), which hold no address and no MAC.

The run: 63 sessions on 4 parallel benches, 28 November 2026, 15:59–16:37, all 63 saved, no evaluation errors. Each session has a base of 0–60 s, a mark at 60 s, a window of 60–120 s and STOP at 127 s.

### 2.3 Limitations of the bench

- **Few independent realizations.** The endpoints are seeded, so sessions of one kind reproduce nearly the same exchanges. The three "resolver down at the mark" sessions had 66 and 50 questions and 46 dropped, all three. The Wilson bounds above assume independent windows, so they **overstate the confidence**. The bench shows behaviour on real exchanges; it does not estimate field rates.
- **DHCP recall is not measured.** The bench's clients never reached the frozen material threshold (+0.3 DISCOVERs a second; they reached +0.12 to +0.22), so no DHCP window counts as a material fault. The engine said CAN'T TELL on all eight DHCP fault windows: no false claim and no false reassurance, but no recall either.
- **One host and one link type (veth).** The endpoints were written for the bench. Counter-read failures were not exercised: the helper's counters cannot be made to fail from outside. No Wi-Fi.
- **Not G0.** G0's criteria are unchanged and G0 rests on its synthetic validations. Nothing here is G0 evidence, field validation or G3/G6 admission.

## 3. Findings from the bench

| # | Finding | Severity | Handling in N1.2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **E2** | **Answers that return by another path read as fewer answers.** With the resolver's route back to the device moved to a second link for part of the window, the engine said `dns.answered` WORSE with quality **good** in 4 of 4 sessions (66 of 66, then 22–31 of 50 answers seen), although the resolver answered every question. The engine's one-way gate (`ONE_WAY`) fires only when *nothing* comes back to the device. Here the ping's replies still came back, so the visibility of one protocol's return path was invisible to it. The synthetic generators never modelled a partial asymmetry, and G0 could not find this. | **Fundamental for verdicts on `dns.answered`.** A device-level visibility gate cannot tell answers that were not sent from answers that took another path | Verdicts stay off. The shell never says ANSWERED: the metric is `DNS ANSWERS SEEN`, its figures are `66 OF 66 → 31 OF 50 QUESTIONS`, and where fewer were seen than before the line adds `AN ANSWER BY ANOTHER PATH IS NOT SEEN HERE`. Before a `dns.answered` verdict can be shown, the engine needs a per-peer return-path check (for example, nothing from the resolver reached the device in the window, though it did before → CAN'T TELL). That means a new freeze and a new held-out validation |
| **E1** | **A wrong reason on bursty rates.** The rate test runs a conservative test with the practical bound and an aggressive one without it. When only the aggressive one rejects, the engine says "the frames lost could account for it", even in a window that lost nothing. One ARP storm starting at 89.9 s (99 → 399 requests, no frame lost) was CAN'T TELL with that reason | Wrong text, safe verdict (CAN'T TELL) | The shell says `TOO BURSTY TO TELL A CHANGE FROM ORDINARY VARIATION` when the comparison carries no frames-lost note. The engine's reason is to be corrected at its next freeze |
| E3 | Abstention on DHCP is total on this bench (8 of 8 fault windows, all below the material threshold) | Expected from the design; DHCP recall is unknown | Said in the figures and the evidence line |

**No fundamental blocker for an observation-only WHEN.** The figures are counts of what was seen, and the evidence line and SEEING say how much was seen. With E2's wording, nothing on the face claims more than the capture saw. E2 is a fundamental limitation of automatic verdicts on DNS answers. It is reported here, and verdicts stay off.

## 4. Integration

### 4.1 The helper (`tools/capture/pos_capture.c`)

- The engine is linked into `pos-capture` only (`POS_CAPTURE_OBJS` has `PPC_INTEL_OBJS`; `-lm`). It is never in the shell, and the lint checks this.
- One allocation at START, beside N1.0's evidence history, **before the privilege drop and the seccomp filter**. Nothing is allocated afterwards. It is freed at START's undo and at the next START.
- It is fed where N1.0's history is fed:
  - every frame, before the shed check, with its direction;
  - every observation, by the network and DNS tables;
  - the 250 ms tick, with the watched / HOLD / shedding state;
  - every lost-frame reading, made or not, through `read_drops`;
  - MARK;
  - STOP.
- **Direction on a live capture** (`core/pocketpcap/ppc_source_pcap.c`). libpcap gives no packet type, so a frame's direction is now taken from the interface's MAC:
  - source is the device: OUTGOING;
  - destination is the device: HOST;
  - all-ones: BROADCAST;
  - group bit set: MULTICAST;
  - otherwise: OTHERHOST.

  Before this, a live capture never marked an answer as the device's own, so every DNS comparison would have abstained live. N1.0's evidence page now gets the same classification.

### 4.2 Protocol 7

- `intel <req> <mark> <pair>`: mark 0 is the newest; pair 1 is after the mark against before it, pair 0 is before the mark against its reference.
- The answer is queued whole and never logged: `in.begin <req> <mark> <pair> <at_ms> <now_ms> <final>`, two `in.vis` (the base, then the window: watched, HOLD, counted-only and not-held seconds, frames, losses as a bound, whether the losses are known, the device's own frames in and out), five `in.cmp`, `in.end <req>`.
- Each `in.cmp` carries the metric, verdict, quality, reason, notes, the two n, k, statistics and undecided counts, the p-value ×1e6, and up to four packet references as `seq:alive`.
- The widest answer is 816 bytes (`ppc_proto_test`); every line is under 512 bytes. A mark that is not held is refused with `error nomark`.
- `capture.status` (Companion) is unchanged, and the lint checks this.
- The shell and the helper are installed together. The shell refuses a helper of another protocol.

### 4.3 The shell: WHEN, observation-only (DS §59.12, §59.13)

- `apps/packetshark/psk_in.[ch]` holds the words:
  - the figures;
  - the evidence line;
  - SEEING;
  - the summary;
  - packet references with their state.

  `PSK_IN_VERDICTS` is `0`, so no verdict word is ever shown.
- The model keeps an answer only when it is whole (two `in.vis`, all five `in.cmp`) and answers the newest request of its pair. A new capture forgets it.
- The session asks for both pairs with each request for a mark's evidence.
- **WHEN is a panel at the top of a mark's evidence page.** It shows:
  - the summary (`5 OBSERVED · 2 WITH LIMITS`, `WINDOW STILL FILLING …`) and `OBSERVATIONS ONLY, NO VERDICTS YET`;
  - SEEING, in the warning colour when anything limited the view;
  - the five comparisons: figures, the evidence line (warning colour unless both windows were watched whole), and packet references (warning colour when one has expired);
  - pair A on one line.
- Deviations from the approved mockups (D8), each with its reason, are in DS §59.13. The main ones:
  - no verdicts;
  - no INSIGHT row on LIVE (the count of marks leads to the page);
  - SEEING in words, not as a drawn strip;
  - no lens chips.
- N1.0 MARK and its evidence page are unchanged around the new panel.

## 6. Security

- **F1 is unchanged:**
  - the identity switch, the user and network namespaces, `PR_SET_NO_NEW_PRIVS`;
  - the seccomp allowlist;
  - the capture-directory rules and `fs.protected_*`.

  The engine adds no system call after the drop. Its one allocation is made before it, and the lint checks the order.
- **Bounded:** the engine has fixed rings and a fixed ledger; the answer is at most 816 bytes; requests are parsed strictly (four fields, pair ≤ 1).
- **Privacy:**
  - HEADERS is unchanged;
  - the engine keeps counts, times, packet numbers and loss readings, no name and no payload;
  - the answer carries packet numbers, never addresses;
  - nothing of it is logged.
- **The bench** ran in isolated namespaces with owned endpoints. Raw captures are outside Git.

## 7. What is not validated

- **G1 on a unit.** The engine's cost on Unit A or Unit B, and the helper's memory there.
- **Live WHEN on hardware**, including the MAC-based direction on the unit's Ethernet and Wi-Fi.
- **The layouts on the device** (AMOLED, finger).
- **Field traffic** (G3/G6), DHCP recall, counter-read failures, Wi-Fi.
- **The E2 return-path check**, and the E1 reason fix. Both need a new engine freeze and a new held-out validation before verdicts can be considered.
