**Criteria set by the owner on 27 November 2026 and frozen in pull request #196, before any held-out validation.** **First held-out result (28 November 2026): not passed** (§6): the synthetic held-out run passes; the independent generator fails four of six categories for every rule, the loss-aware rule included. **Recovery and second freeze (28 November 2026)** (§7): the causes corrected in the C engine, G0 now scored on the C engine itself, the criteria unchanged. **Second held-out result: not passed** (§7.5). The synthetic run passes; the second independent generator fails every category and recall. The cause is a modelling limitation the development generators could not show. **Round 5 (28 November 2026)** (§8): Unit A's traffic measured, the count and silence models replaced and independently reviewed, a third freeze before one held-out run. **Third held-out result: G0 passes** (§8.6), on E3a-R's new seeds and on the third independent generator. That is synthetic evidence, not field accuracy. Changing a criterion, a definition or one of the engine's decision values after a held-out result has been seen invalidates that held-out run. This page replaces G0's earlier wording in [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md) §2 ("≥ 99 % of affected intervals produce no PROBLEM claim", with a recall-under-loss threshold still to be set). That wording could be passed by an engine that always abstains.

G0 protects the brief's first question: is a reply slow, or is it simply not visible from here?

## 1. Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Problem claim** | A WORSE verdict on any of the five comparisons of a mark ([N1.1.md](N1.1.md) §4): DNS answer time, DNS questions answered, DNS failures, DHCP DISCOVER rate, ARP request rate. |
| **False problem claim** | A problem claim on a metric whose true rate did not change in the harmful direction. A claim of a real benign rise (for example more ARP requests) is not false. |
| **Healthy window** | A comparison window in which no fault was injected. |
| **Healthy visibility-artifact categories** | Each healthy window is classed by what limited the capture's view of it (it may fall in two): **clean** (no artifact, counters read); **uniform loss** (0.1–60 % of frames lost, independent of kind); **burst loss** (time-correlated: drops in busy seconds of every kind, when answers are also slow); **HOLD**; **one-sided visibility** (this device sends, nothing reaches it); **counters failed** (a spell of failed lost-frame readings over the window). |
| **Material fault** | One whose effect exceeds the changes the engine treats as material: the resolver stops answering (≥ 70 % of answers missing), the resolver slows ≥ 3 times, SERVFAIL on ≥ 30 % of answers, ARP requests ≥ 5 times, DHCP DISCOVERs +0.3 a second or more. A subtle slowdown of 1.3–1.8 times is **not** material and is reported apart. |
| **Overlapping capture loss** | A fault in a window that also has uniform or burst capture loss. |

**What failed: one claim, two mechanisms** (read-only diagnosis, `diagnose.py`; no rule was changed). Every false claim on the independent set is "more ARP requests":

1. **A hidden receive path in the base minute.** The independent generator's one-sided artifact also hides broadcast frames. When it falls in the base minute, the base's ARP count is too low and the window looks like a rise. The engine's one-way rules guard only the DNS comparisons. The lost-frame counter does not see these frames, because they never reached the interface. This accounts for the false claims in uniform loss, HOLD and most of failed counters (each of those windows had a one-sided base minute).
2. **Burst loss weighted by traffic.** The loss share is lost frames over all frames. Bursts of extra traffic, dropped at high rates, put it at about 50 % in such windows, while ARP requests, spread over the minute, lost about 10–15 %. The rate test then expects far fewer ARP requests than arrive and calls the real count a rise. The engine's assumption that a stream spread evenly in time suffers the window's traffic-weighted loss share is wrong for time-correlated loss. E3a-R's own burst model has the same bias, but mildly: its busy seconds triple the traffic, so the traffic-weighted share overstates the ARP stream's loss a little. The independent generator's bursts add 200–3,000 frames a second, which overstates it several times over.

**What it means.** The engine passes on the generator it was developed with, and on new seeds of it, but not on an independent one. That is exactly what held-out validation exists to catch. The N1.1 engine is **not validated**, and its rate verdicts must not be shown as claims.

Fixes are possible (design notes, not applied):

- apply the one-sided rules, or a receive-gap check on all frames seen, to the rate comparisons too;
- for a "more" claim on a rate, correct for loss with a time-weighted share at that stream's own arrival times, or not at all.

Any fix needs **a new freeze and new held-out data**, including a fresh independent generator. Re-running this one after fixing against it would be tuning to it.

### 7.2 What was corrected

| Cause (§6 and the owner's brief) | Correction in `ppc_intel.c` |
| --- | --- |
| **Incomplete baseline visibility.** A receive path that stops hides broadcasts as well as this device's replies, and the lost-frame counter does not see it. | `recv_gaps()`: for the counted streams (ARP, DHCP), a run of watched seconds is taken as not seen, like seconds on HOLD, when it meets all of these: no frame arrived except this device's own; the readings show nothing lost; the run is longer than the busier window's arrival rate makes plausible (P ≤ 1/1000). Both rates are counted over the seconds seen. A gap over half a window is CAN'T TELL (one-way). A second that lost frames, or whose losses are not known, neither lengthens nor breaks a run; the loss bounds speak for it. |
| **Selective and burst loss.** The traffic-weighted loss share was applied to a stream spread in time. | `decide_rate()`: a claim of **more** events stands on the events seen; the window's losses are never used to make a rise. The base is taken at its highest: what was seen plus the most the stream may have lost. |
| **Unjustified assumptions about how loss falls on each kind.** | `stream_lost()`: what a stream may have lost is estimated at its own arrival seconds. Each second's lost frames, attributed to it by the readings, are multiplied by the stream's part of that second's frames. The assumption is that kernel drops are by arrival time, not by kind, at one-second resolution. The larger of this and the traffic-weighted share bounds each side. "Fewer" and "no change" take the window at its highest. |
| **False reassurance when evidence is insufficient.** | `decide_share()`: questions left undecided count against each verdict. For "fewer answered" they count as answered; for "more" and "no change" they count as unanswered, since only answered questions are always decided. For failures, the answers not seen count as failures for "fewer failures" and "no change". |

No decision value changed: alpha, the minimum counts, the material changes, the answer-time bounds and the silent-spell probability are as in §3.

- The visibility corrections of §7.2 hold: the second generator's artifact categories add little over its clean rate.

- The engine stays host-only and is not linked into `pos-capture`.

**What a further round would need** (design notes, not applied; each needs the owner's decision, a new freeze and a third independent generator):

- a count test robust to overdispersion, for example a dispersion estimated from the per-second counts of both windows, or a negative binomial or permutation test over seconds;
- `one_way_spell()` treating loss-explained seconds as `recv_gaps()` does;
- a measurement of real per-second ARP and DHCP burstiness on Unit A, from its own traffic, before choosing the model.

### 8.2 The method (as frozen)

1. **Rates of broadcasts (ARP, DHCP): quasi-Poisson, with a practical bound on rises** (`rate_dispersion()`, `decide_rate()`).
   - **Dispersion φ:** each window's seconds seen (watched whole; base receive gaps taken out) are cut into blocks of `PPC_IN_DISP_BLOCK_S` = 5 s. A short last block joins the one before. The variance of the block counts is taken about each window's own mean, pooled over both windows, and divided by the mean block count.
     - It is a point estimate, never below 1.
     - Fewer than 4 pooled degrees of freedom is CAN'T TELL (too few).
     - Counts and expectations in every tail are divided by φ, and the base's Z-bound is widened by √φ.
   - **The practical bound:** a rise is a worsening only from `PPC_IN_RISE_PCT` = 1.5 times the base's highest, as an answer time is only from 1.5 times.
     - A significant rise below that is SMALL, and only where a material rise is ruled out as for NO CHANGE. Otherwise it is CAN'T TELL.
     - The bound stands for variation at the scale of a whole minute, which no block inside a minute can see. Material faults are 3 times or more.
   - **Counting:** events are counted in watched seconds only. A receive gap leaves the base, where its seconds would make a rise. It does not leave the window after, where taking out quiet seconds would select on the outcome. "Better" and "no change" use the window's seconds seen.
   - **Why 5 s:** what conservatism needs is that φ at the block length is at least φ at the window's length. That is 2.56–2.92 against 0.16–0.22 on Unit A. Both 2 s and 5 s meet it; 5 s is the more conservative and costs almost no recall (§8.3). Bursts or periods longer than about 10–20 s raise φ less, and the practical bound covers them.
   - **Why not a negative binomial or a permutation test:**
     - A negative binomial assumes an over-dispersed shape that Unit A's data contradict at the comparison scale.
     - Rank or permutation tests over 12 blocks have little power at sparse rates and do not carry the loss bounds.
     - Quasi-Poisson keeps the decision's structure, its loss bounds and a cost of a few dozen additions a comparison.
2. **Silence: how often a second carries a frame, not the mean rate** (`silence_hazard()`).
   - A quiet run is too long when (1 − p)^run ≤ 1/1000, with p the share of the busier window's seconds that carried a frame, taken at its lower confidence bound and thinned by loss.
   - Arrivals come in bursts: a transfer, then quiet. The mean rate made short quiet spells look impossible; that turned 70 % of the second generator's DNS faults under loss into CAN'T TELL (one-way).
   - The rule is used by `one_way_spell()` (DNS) and `recv_gaps()` (broadcasts).
3. **DNS one-way, loss-aware:**
   - A second whose silence the readings explain, or whose losses are not known, neither lengthens nor breaks a run.
   - **The way back must be seen within a second of a question** (`PPC_IN_WITNESS_S` = 1). A frame after a one-sided spell had ended said nothing of an answer due inside it.
   - A question without that witness stays undecided, which counts against every verdict (§7.2). Abstention is kept where loss and a missing return path cannot be told apart.

The frozen decision values of §3 are unchanged. New values: the 5-s block, from the measurement; the 1.5× rise bound, by the answer times' precedent; 1 s for the witness; and 4 degrees of freedom.

**What this does and does not establish.**

- **It does:** on three independently written models of networks and capture artifacts, plus new seeds of the original one, the engine **almost never claims a problem** that a degraded view could have made up, and it **finds most material faults** that overlap capture loss.
- **It does not:** speak for real networks. §4 step 3, replays of consented bench captures (the G3/G6 corpus), is still required before any claim about field behaviour. The traffic measured on Unit A (§8.1) shows what to expect there: periodic ARP, more regular over a minute than Poisson. It contained no DHCP and no fault.
- **Abstention stays high:** 36–47 % of material faults. Most of it is DNS on devices that ask too few questions. Unit A asked none during the measurement. The INSIGHT/WHEN face must make CAN'T TELL and its reason prominent, not an afterthought.
- **The known recall risk is not disproved:** faults that start inside the minute widen the dispersion. Two of the three independent generators started faults at the mark, and the third covered 85–100 % of the minute.
