# PacketShark NEURAL: G0, visibility honesty — frozen acceptance criteria

**Criteria set by the owner on 27 November 2026 and frozen in pull request #196, before any held-out validation.** **First held-out result (28 November 2026): not passed** (§6): the synthetic held-out run passes; the independent generator fails four of six categories for every rule, the loss-aware rule included. **Recovery and second freeze (28 November 2026)** (§7): the causes corrected in the C engine, G0 now scored on the C engine itself, the criteria unchanged. **Second held-out result: not passed** (§7.5). The synthetic run passes; the second independent generator fails every category and recall. The cause is a modelling limitation the development generators could not show. Changing a criterion, a definition or one of the engine's decision values after a held-out result has been seen invalidates that held-out run. This page replaces G0's earlier wording in [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md) §2 ("≥ 99 % of affected intervals produce no PROBLEM claim", with a recall-under-loss threshold still to be set). That wording could be passed by an engine that always abstains.

G0 protects the brief's first question: is a reply slow, or is it simply not visible from here?

## 1. Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Problem claim** | A WORSE verdict on any of the five comparisons of a mark ([N1.1.md](N1.1.md) §4): DNS answer time, DNS questions answered, DNS failures, DHCP DISCOVER rate, ARP request rate. |
| **False problem claim** | A problem claim on a metric whose true rate did not change in the harmful direction. A claim of a real benign rise (for example more ARP requests) is not false. |
| **Healthy window** | A comparison window in which no fault was injected. |
| **Healthy visibility-artifact categories** | Each healthy window is classed by what limited the capture's view of it (it may fall in two): **clean** (no artifact, counters read); **uniform loss** (0.1–60 % of frames lost, independent of kind); **burst loss** (time-correlated: drops in busy seconds of every kind, when answers are also slow); **HOLD**; **one-sided visibility** (this device sends, nothing reaches it); **counters failed** (a spell of failed lost-frame readings over the window). |
| **Material fault** | One whose effect exceeds the changes the engine treats as material: the resolver stops answering (≥ 70 % of answers missing), the resolver slows ≥ 3 times, SERVFAIL on ≥ 30 % of answers, ARP requests ≥ 5 times, DHCP DISCOVERs +0.3 a second or more. A subtle slowdown of 1.3–1.8 times is **not** material and is reported apart. |
| **Overlapping capture loss** | A fault in a window that also has uniform or burst capture loss. |

Under the frozen protocol (§4: G0 passes when both runs pass), **G0 is not passed.**

**What failed: one claim, two mechanisms** (read-only diagnosis, `diagnose.py`; no rule was changed). Every false claim on the independent set is "more ARP requests":

1. **A hidden receive path in the base minute.** The independent generator's one-sided artifact also hides broadcast frames. When it falls in the base minute, the base's ARP count is too low and the window looks like a rise. The engine's one-way rules guard only the DNS comparisons. The lost-frame counter does not see these frames, because they never reached the interface. This accounts for the false claims in uniform loss, HOLD and most of failed counters (each of those windows had a one-sided base minute).
2. **Burst loss weighted by traffic.** The loss share is lost frames over all frames. Bursts of extra traffic, dropped at high rates, put it at about 50 % in such windows, while ARP requests, spread over the minute, lost about 10–15 %. The rate test then expects far fewer ARP requests than arrive and calls the real count a rise. The engine's assumption that a stream spread evenly in time suffers the window's traffic-weighted loss share is wrong for time-correlated loss. E3a-R's own burst model has the same bias, but mildly: its busy seconds triple the traffic, so the traffic-weighted share overstates the ARP stream's loss a little. The independent generator's bursts add 200–3,000 frames a second, which overstates it several times over.

**What it means.** The engine passes on the generator it was developed with, and on new seeds of it, but not on an independent one. That is exactly what held-out validation exists to catch. The N1.1 engine is **not validated**, and its rate verdicts must not be shown as claims.

Fixes are possible (design notes, not applied):

- apply the one-sided rules, or a receive-gap check on all frames seen, to the rate comparisons too;
- for a "more" claim on a rate, correct for loss with a time-weighted share at that stream's own arrival times, or not at all.

Any fix needs **a new freeze and new held-out data**, including a fresh independent generator. Re-running this one after fixing against it would be tuning to it.

### 7.2 What was corrected

| Cause (§6 and the owner's brief) | Correction in `ppc_intel.c` |
| --- | --- |
| **Incomplete baseline visibility.** A receive path that stops hides broadcasts as well as this device's replies, and the lost-frame counter does not see it. | `recv_gaps()`: for the counted streams (ARP, DHCP), a run of watched seconds is taken as not seen, like seconds on HOLD, when it meets all of these: no frame arrived except this device's own; the readings show nothing lost; the run is longer than the busier window's arrival rate makes plausible (P ≤ 1/1000). Both rates are counted over the seconds seen. A gap over half a window is CAN'T TELL (one-way). A second that lost frames, or whose losses are not known, neither lengthens nor breaks a run; the loss bounds speak for it. |
| **Selective and burst loss.** The traffic-weighted loss share was applied to a stream spread in time. | `decide_rate()`: a claim of **more** events stands on the events seen; the window's losses are never used to make a rise. The base is taken at its highest: what was seen plus the most the stream may have lost. |
| **Unjustified assumptions about how loss falls on each kind.** | `stream_lost()`: what a stream may have lost is estimated at its own arrival seconds. Each second's lost frames, attributed to it by the readings, are multiplied by the stream's part of that second's frames. The assumption is that kernel drops are by arrival time, not by kind, at one-second resolution. The larger of this and the traffic-weighted share bounds each side. "Fewer" and "no change" take the window at its highest. |
| **False reassurance when evidence is insufficient.** | `decide_share()`: questions left undecided count against each verdict. For "fewer answered" they count as answered; for "more" and "no change" they count as unanswered, since only answered questions are always decided. For failures, the answers not seen count as failures for "fewer failures" and "no change". |

No decision value changed: alpha, the minimum counts, the material changes, the answer-time bounds and the silent-spell probability are as in §3.

**Reading:**

- Real ARP on this network comes in bursts of one or two seconds. It is over-dispersed at block lengths up to about 10 s, and the dispersion peaks at 5 s.
- Over a whole minute it is more regular than Poisson (periodic announcements and probes), so a minute's sum varies less than a Poisson count's.
- The failure mode of §7.5, false rises between healthy minutes, did not occur on this hour of real traffic, even with the old test. The second independent generator's bursts are not what this network does. They are still a plausible network, so the engine must survive them.

**Limitations.**

- The installed software reports frames by class, not by kind. These are **all ARP frames** (requests, plus the replies a station sees: its own and broadcast ones) and **all DHCP frames**, not ARP requests and DHCP DISCOVERs. On a switch port or a Wi-Fi station others' unicast replies are not delivered, so ARP frames are mostly requests, but the split is not measured.
- Inter-arrival times below one second cannot be seen: per-second counts are rebuilt from polls about 1 s apart, spread by overlap. That smooths the 1-second figures and adds some lag-1 correlation. The figures at 2 s and longer are not affected.
- One network, one hour per medium, one time of day.
- DHCP could not be characterized: none occurred.

### 8.2 The method

Three of the engine's statistical assumptions were replaced. Each replacement estimates variability from the windows themselves rather than assuming a distribution.

1. **Rates of broadcasts (ARP, DHCP): quasi-Poisson with a dispersion estimated from 5-second blocks** (`rate_dispersion()`, `decide_rate()`).
   - Each window's seconds actually seen are cut into blocks of `PPC_IN_DISP_BLOCK_S` = 5 s, in time order. "Seen" means watched whole and outside receive gaps.
   - The variance of the block counts about **each window's own mean** is pooled over the two windows, so that a change at the mark is not mistaken for spread, and divided by the mean block count.
   - The estimate φ is taken at its one-sided 95 % upper bound, χ² with the pooled degrees of freedom (Wilson–Hilferty). It is never below 1.
   - Every bound and tail of the rate decision uses φ: the counts and expectations are divided by φ (rounded the way that favours the null), and the base's Z-bound is widened by √φ.
   - With fewer than 4 pooled degrees of freedom the comparison is CAN'T TELL (too few).
   - **Why 5 s:** it is the shortest block that absorbs every burst Unit A showed (busy runs ≤ 2 s, quiet runs ≤ 4 s, dispersion peaking at 5 s), and it leaves the most degrees of freedom. On Unit A's traffic it overstates a minute's variance (2.56 against 0.22), which is conservative. Bursts longer than 5 s would be under-counted; the upper bound and the floor of 1 do not cover that.
   - **Why not a negative binomial or a permutation test:**
     - A negative binomial adds a shape assumption that Unit A's data do not support: they are under-dispersed at the comparison scale.
     - A permutation or rank test over 12 blocks a window has little power at the sparse rates where faults matter (DHCP), and does not carry the loss bounds of §7.2.
     - Quasi-Poisson keeps the decision's structure, its loss bounds and its cost: a few dozen additions a comparison.
2. **Silence: how often a second carries a frame, not the mean rate** (`silence_hazard()`, used by `one_way_spell()` and `recv_gaps()`).
   - A run of silent seconds is too long when (1 − p)^run ≤ 1/1000, where p is the share of the busier window's seconds that carried at least one frame, thinned by loss.
   - In the second generator, the device's inbound traffic arrives as bulk bursts (about 900 frames a second for a few seconds, then nothing). The mean rate made every short quiet spell look impossible; that is where 70 % of its DNS faults under loss became CAN'T TELL (one-way).
3. **DNS one-way, loss-aware.**
   - In `one_way_spell()`, a second whose silence the readings explain (frames lost in it), or whose losses are not known, neither lengthens nor breaks a run, as in `recv_gaps()`.
   - **The way back must be seen working when the answer was due:** in the question's second or the next (`PPC_IN_WITNESS_S` = 1), not anywhere in the 5-second wait. A frame after a one-sided spell ended said nothing of an answer due inside it.
   - Questions without that witness stay undecided. That counts against every verdict (§7.2), so abstention is kept where loss and a missing return path cannot be told apart.

No criterion and no frozen decision value of §3 changed (alpha, minimum counts, material changes, answer-time bounds). The three new values have stated reasons, not tuned ones: 5 s from Unit A's measurement, the 95 % bound as the usual confidence level, and 1 s as the latest a typical answer is due.
