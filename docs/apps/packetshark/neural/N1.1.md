## 2. Replay fixtures (`tests/ppc_intel_fixtures.h`)

Each scene is 4 min 40 s of a scripted capture on a **virtual clock**: engine time is packet time. A mark is set at 200 s, and the capture stops at 280 s. Every scene has the same background:

- a keepalive to and from a server every second (this device's own traffic in both directions);
- one DNS question every second;
- an ARP request from the router every 10 s.

Frames are built by N1.0's builders, byte for byte the same on every run. They use documentation addresses only.

**Readings.** The counter is read every 250 ms, as the helper does, and some readings fail. Capture loss removes frames by a fixed hash of their position, and the readings report exactly those frames. HOLD drops frames before analysis, as the helper does.

**Pairs.** Each mark gets two comparison pairs:

- **Pair A:** the minute before the mark against the two minutes before that.
- **Pair B:** the minute after the mark against the minute before it.

| Scene | What happens | The engine's ledger (VERIFIED, `tests/data/packetshark/intel/*.ledger`) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `slow-dns` | Answers slow from about 30 to about 420 ms at the mark | Pair A: no change in any of the five metrics. Pair B: DNS answer time **worse** (median 30.0 → 419.5 ms, 60 \| 60 answers, quality good); answered 60 of 60 → 60 of 60, **no change** |
| `one-sided` | From 140 s every reply to this device takes another path | DNS time, answered and failed: **can't tell, one-way**, in both pairs. ARP and DHCP (broadcast, still seen): no change |
| `hold-recovery` | The resolver stops at 170 s. HOLD runs from 205 to 225 s, and the resolver answers again at 215 s, inside the HOLD. | Pair A: answered **worse** (120 of 120 → 30 of 60, good). Pair B: answered **better** (30 of 60 → 35 of 35), degraded, noted *unwatched* (21 s on HOLD) and *undecided* (5 questions whose wait met HOLD were left out, not called unanswered). DHCP DISCOVER **can't tell, weak**: 39 watched seconds cannot rule out a rise of 6 a minute |
| `loss-healthy` | A quarter of all frames is lost after the mark while DNS answers every question. The counters cannot be read from 190 to 258 s, so the loss is known in total but not where it fell (a lagged signal). | Pair B: answered **can't tell, loss-explains** (60 of 60 → 40 of 52, loss bounds 0–56 in both windows). Answer time no change (loss does not bias the time of an answer that was seen). No false claim anywhere |
| `fault-under-loss` | The resolver stops at 205 s under the same 25 % loss | Pair B: answered **worse** (60 of 60 → 2 of 45), degraded, noted *loss*. A real fault is still claimed under loss (recall under loss). Answer time can't tell, too few (2 pairs) |
| `loss-unknown` | Slow answers after the mark. The counters cannot be read from 250 s to the end. The buffer is small (128 summaries, 8 KiB). | Pair A, made final before the counters failed, is untouched. Pair B: answer time **worse** (degraded: loss does not bias a time). DHCP and ARP: **can't tell, loss-unknown** (unknown loss could hide a rise). Answered 60 of 60 → 60 of 60 stays **no change** (lost frames cannot hide fewer answers). The packets the comparison references have left the buffer and say so (`x` gone, `s` summary only) |

`tests/ppc_intel_test.c` (VERIFIED, 64 checks) also checks:

- **Expected ledgers.** Each scene's ledger matches its expected file byte for byte. The verdicts that matter are also asserted by name, so an expected file cannot bless a wrong answer.
- **Determinism.** Each scene gives the same ledger twice, and with the clock ticked every 1 s, 250 ms or 50 ms.
- **Replay parity on the host.** The scene's surviving frames are written as a pcap, with the readings, HOLD, marks and STOP in a `.vis` file beside it. Read back with direction taken from this device's MAC, they replay to the same ledger.
- **What the loss signal prevents.** `loss-healthy` played with counters that report nothing lost claims *fewer answered*, a false claim. With every reading made, it says **no change** on degraded evidence: the loss is then known exactly, and it accounts for the answers that were not seen.
- **The statistics against known values,** and the placement of the loss bound: a reading inside a window counts in its lower bound, and one that straddles a window's end counts in its upper bound only.
- **The bounds.** A 40-minute capture with five questions a second, a mark every 30 s and a loss at every reading wraps every ring. Ids are never reused. A window the rings no longer hold abstains with *history*.

The fixtures cover the five cases of the brief: slow DNS replies, one-sided visibility, HOLD across recovery, packet loss during healthy exchanges, and real faults overlapping capture loss. They add a sixth for unknown loss and expired packets.

## 3. E3a redone: E3a-R (`research/packetshark-neural/e3a-r/`)

**Synthetic only.** These figures show how decision rules behave on traffic a generator written for this study invented. They are **not field accuracy** and must not be cited as such (G3, G6 and G7 need the consented corpus). The generator, the rules and the scoring share one author. The windows below are G0's **development set**: the rules were tuned on them ([G0.md](G0.md) §3–4).

### What changed from E3a (review F7, HANDOVER §4 risk 4)

- **Coverage.** No method is handed the true loss. Coverage is the helper's own signal: a cumulative lost-frame counter read every 250 ms. In 35 % of windows, readings fail in spells of 1–40 s. A reading reports what was lost since the last good one, not when it was lost.
- **Loss.** Loss is drawn log-uniformly from 0.1 % to 60 %, over 8–60 s. Half of the loss artifacts are **burst (time-correlated) loss**: in about 30 % of their seconds the path is busy (three times the traffic), the kernel drops frames of every kind at 30–80 %, and answers are 2–5 times slower. Slow answers are therefore lost more often than the window's loss share says. This is the selective loss bias the owner asked to be addressed (§3, point 4).
- **One-way visibility** is inferred from this device's frames in and out, never given.
- **Overlap.** Faults (50 % of windows) and artifacts (50 %; loss, HOLD or one-way) are drawn independently, so about a quarter of the windows hold both.
- **Seeds.** Ten seeds (1000–1009), with 40 sites × 60 windows each, for 24,000 windows in all.
- **Methods.** Deterministic methods only. Training is excluded by this brief, so E3a's learned rows are not repeated.
- **Rules.** The rules are the engine's own (`ppc_intel.c`), mirrored line by line, and the statistics self-test against the C test's values.

### Methods

| Method | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **naive** | No visibility handling |
| **gate** | E3a's binary gate on the *estimated* per-second coverage (drop seconds above 2 % loss, and HOLD) |
| **oracle-gate** | The same gate on the *true* loss: E3a's construction |
| **loss-aware** | The N1.1 engine |

### What it shows, within a generator's limits

1. **E3a's gate figure was constructed, and the honest picture is narrower.** The binary gate keeps false alerts under loss near the clean rate. It pays with recall when a fault overlaps loss: 44 % against naive's 59 %. The gate on the true loss does no better. Loss-aware estimation keeps the gate's false-alert rate and decides 56.5 % of all faults under loss. On material faults it decides 65.8 %.
2. **One-sided visibility that starts inside a window defeats a whole-window rule.** Both gates alert on about 42 % of one-way windows. Three rules close this:
   - *Nothing came back at all.*
   - A *silent spell* that this device's inbound rate makes implausible (P ≤ 1/1000). The rate is taken from the busier of the two windows, so a long silence cannot lower its own yardstick.
   - A question is decided unanswered only if a frame other than a DNS answer **reached this device during its wait**: the way back was seen working. Otherwise it is undecided.

   Together they bring one-way false alerts to 0.1 %. **The cost is real:** where a fault overlaps one-way visibility, the engine abstains (recall 36 % against about 50 %). The gates' extra "detections" there come from the same mechanism that gives them 42 % false alerts. A resolver that stops and replies that take another path look the same from this port when DNS answers are this device's only inbound traffic. The engine says so instead of guessing.
3. **"No change" must be earned.** "No change" also needs the window to rule out a material worsening:
   - the answered share halving;
   - the failure share 25 points higher;
   - a rate three times higher, or 6 a minute more;
   - for answer time, fewer than half the answers above 1.5 times and 20 ms.

   Otherwise the engine abstains with *weak*. A significant difference below the practical bound is *small change*, never "no change". False reassurance on material faults is 1.8 %.
4. **Selective loss in answer times is handled conservatively.** When frames drop in busy seconds, and slow answers arrive in those seconds, the answers *seen* are biased fast. A comparison of seen answers would then hide a slowdown. Lost answers and answers slower than the 5 s wait look the same (a question decided unanswered), so the engine counts every unseen answer as **slower than any seen**, and each verdict must survive that:
   - **Slower:** the base's unseen answers count as slow.
   - **Faster:** the window's unseen answers count as slow.
   - **No change:** fewer than half the window's answers above the bound, with its unseen answers counted above it.

   A difference that only the seen answers support is CAN'T TELL with *unseen*. Loss of an answer cannot then make a slowdown look like no change.

   In E3a-R's burst-loss windows without a fault, 55 had a *material* true slowdown, counting every answer the network sent, seen or not. All 55 were CAN'T TELL under both the old and the new rule, so none was falsely reassured. The new rule changes how the windows *without* a material slowdown are worded: "no change" fell from 489 to 453 of 1,099. Two direct tests in `ppc_intel_test.c` pin the rule:
   - half the window's answers never seen gives CAN'T TELL (weak), not "no change";
   - seen answers twice as slow while half the base's answers were never seen gives CAN'T TELL (unseen), not "slower".

   The answered share stays loss-aware by the uniform-loss assumption. A drop mechanism that singled out DNS answers by kind would break it. The first, unphysical version of the burst model did exactly that, and the rule then made false claims (6.4 % of loss windows). Kernel ring drops are by arrival time, not by frame kind (ASSUMED; to be checked against real captures under load).
5. **The unit's own DNS traffic decides what DNS comparisons can say.** With 2–9 questions a minute, they decide 4.5 % of DNS faults. Unit A's measurement (§10) puts that in context. ARP and DHCP, which reach every port, are decided in 96–99 % of their faults.

**What E3a-R changed in the engine before this record:**

| Round | Change |
| --- | --- |
| First (PR #196 as opened) | The whole-window ratio one-way rule replaced (it fired on devices that simply send more than they receive); the power check and *small change* |
| Second (owner's G0 brief) | The answer-time rule against selective loss; the return-path witness; the silent spell's rate from the busier window. Without the last two, one-way false alerts were 1.5 % (the rate over both windows) and 3.7 % (the first version), both above G0's 1 % |

E3a-R also caught bugs in its own generator and scoring during development, all fixed before the figures above:

- a base-minute one-way artifact that ran on into the window;
- a true benign ARP rise scored as a false claim;
- an unphysical kind-selective loss model;
- every short burst scored as a material slowdown.

**Limits.**

- The generator's traffic and faults are invented, and the rules were written knowing them.
- Kernel drops are assumed independent of frame kind within a second.
- One-way visibility is all-or-nothing per device.
- There is no real capture, no unit and no learned model.

**Status of E3a.** E3a's original gate figures remain withdrawn ([EXPERIMENTS.md](EXPERIMENTS.md)); E3a-R replaces them for design purposes only.

## 4. The slice's design

```
 pos-capture (not linked yet)                       host now: tests/ppc_intel_test.c
  on_frame ── ppc_decode ── ppc_signals ─┐           the same calls, on a virtual clock
                         └─ ppc_net ─────┤ ppc_ev_obs (N1.0's observations)
                                         ├──► ppc_evid  (N1.0: rows, windows)       unchanged
                                         └──► ppc_intel (N1.1: series, ledger)      new
  ticks · counter readings · MARK · STOP ──► both
```

- **Reuse.** The engine reads N1.0's 32-byte observations (`ppc_evid_from_dns`, `ppc_evid_from_net`), the ticks and counter readings N1.0 already receives (`read_drops`), and N1.0's mark ids. It needs no new observer, no new decoder path and no new capture state. Each helper call site would gain one line beside N1.0's.
- **Telemetry series keyed by packet time.** 512 one-second records hold:
  - what the capture was doing (watching, HOLD, shedding);
  - frames analysed or shed;
  - this device's frames in and out;
  - its DNS questions, retries, answers and failures;
  - DHCP DISCOVERs and ARP requests.

  Late frames land in their own second. The live loop clock is never used (ENGINE §2).
- **Bounded, session-local evidence ledger.** The ledger holds 128 comparisons, with ids never reused. There are 512 own DNS questions (each with its packet, its answer's packet and the answer time), 256 event references and 512 loss intervals. Everything is one allocation of 54,784 bytes at init (VERIFIED). Nothing is logged, written, sent or kept past the session (D5, ADR-016 rule 8). No address, name or payload byte is kept (lint-pinned).
- **Loss-aware visibility estimation.** Loss is an interval, never a point:
  - A reading whose interval lies inside a window counts in both its lower and upper bound.
  - One that straddles a window's edge counts in the upper bound only.
  - Readings that have not been made bound the loss by 1, and the comparison says the losses are not known.
  - Each test runs at both ends of the bound. A change is claimed only when the end that favours no change rejects. When only the other end rejects, the engine abstains: *loss-explains*, or *loss-unknown*.
  - Seconds on HOLD or shedding are not watched. A question whose 5-second wait met them is left out (*undecided*), never called unanswered.
  - One-sided visibility is detected as in §3, point 2.
- **Deterministic before/after comparisons.** Five metrics:

  | Metric | Test |
  | --- | --- |
  | DNS answer time | Medians and Mann–Whitney, with a 1.5× and 20 ms practical bound |
  | DNS answered share | Binomial, loss-aware |
  | DNS failure share | Binomial |
  | DHCP DISCOVER rate | Poisson, loss-aware |
  | ARP request rate | Poisson, loss-aware |

  Two pairs per mark (A and B, §2). Every number comes from integer counts; the floating-point tests are fixed in order, so a replay gives the same ledger (G5) on the same platform (VERIFIED on x86-64; riscv64 builds but was not run).
- **Explicit evidence quality and abstention reasons.**

  | Kind | Values |
  | --- | --- |
  | Verdicts | WORSE, BETTER, NO CHANGE, SMALL CHANGE, CAN'T TELL |
  | Quality | GOOD, DEGRADED, INSUFFICIENT |
  | Reasons, in order of precedence | not captured, not analyzed, cut, history, not watched, one-way, too few, loss-unknown, loss-explains, weak |
  | Notes | unwatched, loss, undecided, retries, stopped |

  The decision values are **ASSUMED** design values, for the owner to confirm (§8): alpha 0.01, the minimum counts, the material changes and the 1.5× / 20 ms bound.
- **Stable references.** A comparison stores up to four packet sequence numbers: the slowest answers, the questions not answered, the failures, or the first events. Sequence numbers are never reused in a session. At answer time they resolve against the store to *whole*, *summary only* or *gone* (`ppc_intel_alive`, the same rule as N1.0's `ev_alive`). A gone packet keeps its number and says so.
- **What it does not do.** It makes no WHY claim, no Doctor verdict, no diagnosis and no statement about other devices' traffic. It has no model, no KPU and no new payload. It changes nothing in N1.0's page or protocol.

## 10. The unit's own traffic on Unit A

**27 November 2026, owner-authorized, non-destructive, read-only.**

**Setup.**

- **Software.** The build already installed on Unit A, with N1.0's MARK and evidence: `pos-capture` from master `1fef813b`, and the shell from pull request #195.
- **Unit state.** Both interfaces were up on the same /24, each with a default route (Wi-Fi first). The Companion gateway was on and `meshcored` was running: the unit's ordinary state.

- **Left as found.** No traffic was generated and nothing was saved or installed. The sampler was removed afterwards, and the captures stayed in memory only.

The raw samples, the summary and SHA-256 sums are in the artifacts folder outside the repository.

An earlier Wi-Fi capture ended after 5 minutes, when the app left the capture. It showed the same pattern: no DNS questions, 152 ARP frames a minute.

**What it means (VERIFIED for this unit, this network and this half hour; not a corpus):**

1. **At rest, this unit asks the network nothing.** With no DNS questions of its own in either 15 minutes, WHEN's three DNS comparisons would abstain (*too few*) in almost every window. That is the honest outcome E3a-R predicted below 9 questions a minute (4.5 % of DNS faults decided). DNS comparisons become useful only while the unit's own services resolve names, or with mirror-port capture (D9).
2. **Plumbing is the evidence that is there.** About 150 ARP frames a minute on both interfaces is enough for the ARP comparison to rule out, or claim, a material rise every minute. DHCP was silent: a full watched minute with none rules out a rise of 6 a minute, so the rate comparison still says *no change* rather than guessing. DHCP evidence appears only when something joins.
3. **One-sided visibility is real on this unit.** Three TCP handshakes this device began on Wi-Fi were never seen completing on that interface. Both interfaces share one /24 and both have a default route, and ARP flux was documented on Unit A on 25 November 2026, so replies may take the other interface. That is the case the engine's one-way rules and return-path witness exist for. It was not investigated further, because no traffic was to be generated.
4. **Nothing was lost** at 6–8 frames a second, so the loss bounds would be zero and the quality good.

**Consequences for the design** (recommendations; nothing changed):

- **On a quiet unit, WHEN leads with plumbing.** The approved mockups already word DNS abstention ("this device asked 0 questions" fits the CAN'T TELL card). The default view on a quiet unit will mostly show ARP and DHCP rows and DNS as CAN'T TELL.
- **A plumbing comparison of multicast name announcements** (mDNS, LLMNR: 7–49 a minute here, visible from every port) is the obvious next metric, in line with ENGINE's capability matrix. It needs the owner's decision and a G0 re-run.
