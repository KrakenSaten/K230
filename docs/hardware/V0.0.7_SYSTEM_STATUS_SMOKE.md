# PocketOS v0.0.7: the System Status screen on unit A (2026-09-10)

The last block of v0.0.7, and the first one whose subject is a screen rather
than a service. Everything here was driven by an operator at the panel; the
build, the deployment and every remote check were done over SSH alongside.

**Verdict: PASS.** Every value on the screen matched what `system.status`
reported at the same moment, the filters and the unknown semantics behave on a
real display as they do in the unit tests, and both destructive actions were
carried out from the panel end to end - the board rebooted from a tap, and
powered off from a tap.

Five real-display observations came out of it, two of which were fixed before
release - the second only because the operator corrected their own first
answer. None of them is a safety failure and none blocked the smoke; they are
recorded in full below rather than smoothed over.

No image was built and nothing was flashed.

Two of those rows are the interesting ones.

**`sit0` is not on the screen and `1 interface hidden` is.** The six-octet MAC
rule works at the panel: `eth0`, `wlan0` and `wlan1` have real addresses and
appear; `sit0` reports the four-octet `00:00:00:00` and does not. The caption
means the screen never silently disagrees with `pos system status`, which is
not filtered at all.

**There is no `Card` row.** The running build and the flashed image agree, so
the row that exists to flag a mismatch stays out of the way. This is the
opposite of the simulator run, where there was no `/etc/pocketos-release` at
all and the row correctly appeared. Both directions of the conditional are now
observed.

## Live polling and scroll retention

Over the course of the smoke the screen tracked `UPTIME` from `10h 40m` to
`10h 49m` and `CPU` from `1 %` to `0 %` with no interaction.

**`CPU 0 %`** is worth its own line: an idle board renders `0 %` and not an em
dash. Zero is a measurement, unknown is a dash, and the rule holds on the
device.

With the view scrolled part-way down and untouched for 30 seconds, the
operator reported that it **stayed where it was left, with no flicker**. That
is the repaint-in-place design working: the body is rebuilt only when the
shape of the screen changes, so a poll does not throw away where somebody was
reading.

## Stale and recovery

`S50sysd stop` from SSH, with the API's last answer recorded first so the
freeze point was known: `cpu_percent 1.02`, `temperature_c 49.159`.

| Check | Observed |
| --- | --- |
| Indicator | `LIVE` became `STALE 20s`, the age counting up |
| Values | `CPU 1 %`, `TEMP 49.2 °C` - exactly the recorded freeze point |
| Blanking | none; nothing was zeroed or emptied |
| Recovery | `S50sysd start`, and the screen returned to `LIVE` **on its own within about 10 s, with no reopening** |

The shell was never restarted through any of it (pid 483, `restarts=0`).

## A stopped service, and its return

`S60radiod stop` reported a plain `OK`. On the panel the `radiod` row became
`STOPPED` with an em dash where its pid had been, and the `Radio` row's chip
became `--`. `S60radiod start`, and both went back to `RUNNING` and `RX`
without the screen being touched.

## Crash-loop presentation

Exercised on a throwaway service, never on a real one. `crashtest-demo` was
chosen at fourteen characters - the same length as `pocketos-shell`, the
longest real service name - so the row was stressed at its realistic worst
case, running `exit 139` under `POS_SUPERVISE_MAX_RESTARTS=2`.

State reached `crashloop=1 last_exit_code=139 restarts=3`, and the panel
showed, complete and untruncated:

```
crashtest-demo   CRASH LOOP   exit 139 · 3 restarts/60s
```

This is the defect the simulator run had already caught and fixed: the first
version truncated to `exit 7 · 3 restart...`, losing the `/60s` - which is
precisely the half that stops the number being read as a lifetime total. It
fits at device font metrics.

The throwaway service, its state file, its marker and its log were all removed
afterwards; the runtime directory was left holding the three real services.

## The confirmation dialogs

Neither dialog had ever been rendered - the simulator can open a screen but
cannot drive a tap - so this was new ground.

**Restart.** Title `Restart PocketOS?`, body "Services stop and the board
reboots. This takes about 35 seconds.", buttons `Cancel` and `Restart`.
Cancel returned to the screen with nothing changed.

**Power off.** Title `Power off PocketOS?`, and the body read in full off the
panel:

> The board will shut down and cannot be restarted remotely. To switch it back
> on, disconnect USB power for about 30 seconds, then reconnect.

Fully readable, not cut off. That sentence is what 2026-09-10's power-off
validation cost to learn, delivered to the person at the moment they decide.
Cancel again returned with nothing changed.

Throughout both, `grep -c "accepted, acting"` scoped to this boot stayed at
**0**: opening a dialog reaches nothing. The only two `accepted` lines in the
log were the historical ones from the block 2c smoke.

## Restart, from the panel

The operator tapped `Restart`, then `Restart` again. The screen showed
`Restarting...`, the panel cycled, and the board came back to the launcher by
itself.

```
2026-09-10T14:44:13.180Z sysd INFO  reboot accepted, acting in 200 ms
2026-09-10T14:44:13.380Z sysd INFO  reboot: running /sbin/reboot
2026-09-10T14:44:13Z supervise pocketos-shell stopped
2026-09-10T14:44:13Z supervise radiod stopped
2026-09-10T14:44:14Z supervise sysd stopped
```

**200 ms** between the reply and the command, and three clean supervisor exits
- the line only written on a supervisor's own shutdown path, so nothing was
force-killed. After the reboot: fresh `/run`, new pids (sysd 218/234, radiod
240/252, shell 259/273, from 677/688, 728/739, 468/483), `restarts=0`, no
markers, no temp files, identity deltas 0, build `b9203c8` intact, 3/3
running. Reopened, the screen was healthy and showed `0h 01m`.

This is the whole chain in one tap: panel → confirmation → `shell_ipc` → sysd →
`/sbin/reboot` → init → `rcK` → reboot.

## Power off, from the panel

Same again, and the last step of the smoke.

```
2026-09-10T14:47:12.506Z sysd INFO  poweroff accepted, acting in 200 ms
2026-09-10T14:47:12.706Z sysd INFO  poweroff: running /sbin/poweroff
2026-09-10T14:47:12Z supervise pocketos-shell stopped
2026-09-10T14:47:13Z supervise radiod stopped
2026-09-10T14:47:13Z supervise sysd stopped
```

The screen showed `Powering off...`, then went dark with no light at the
Ethernet port. Confirmed down from SSH: 100 % packet loss over 20 pings and
`No route to host`.

USB power disconnected for about 30 seconds and reconnected, per the hardware
observation recorded in V0.0.7_BLOCK2C_SMOKE.md, and the board booted normally.

After the cold boot: fresh `/run`, new pids (219/235, 241/253, 260/274),
`restarts=0`, `crashloop=0`, no markers, no temp files, identity deltas 0,
build `b9203c8` intact, 3/3 running, and the screen healthy with `LIVE`. The
SoC came back at **44.3 °C** against the 48-49 °C it had been running at,
which is the cold-start signature.

## Real-display observations

None blocked the smoke. The first should be fixed before release.

1. **The power-off dialog emphasised the irreversible action. FIXED.** As
   first observed, `Power off` rendered in accent blue and `Cancel` in
   secondary dark, so the button that cannot be undone from a desk was the one
   drawing the eye. Compounding it, `lv_group_focus_obj(cancel)` is a no-op on
   a touch-only panel - there is no input group, so no focus ring - which meant
   "Cancel is the default" was true in the code and invisible on the glass. The
   two-step confirmation itself was sound and nothing fired until confirmed,
   but the hierarchy was backwards for that one dialog.

   Corrected: which button carries the accent is now a decision in
   `system_view_dialog_emphasis()` rather than a fixed style in the LVGL
   callback, so it is unit-tested. A restart keeps the accent on the action -
   it costs thirty-five seconds and undoes itself. A power-off moves the accent
   to `Cancel` and gives the action the restrained treatment, because it costs
   a walk to the bench and thirty seconds with the cable out. The wording of
   both dialogs, the API behaviour and the 200 ms scheduling are untouched.
   Re-verified on unit A from the corrected build (see the closing section).
2. **The instruction on the `Powering off...` screen cannot be read in
   practice.** The operator saw the heading and "some text I did not get to
   read before it was gone" - the board goes down about 200 ms later, so the
   terminal repetition adds nothing. The instruction that matters is the one
   in the dialog, which was read in full before committing. Worth keeping for
   the case where the command fails and the screen stays up, but it should not
   be relied on.
3. **`Model` dot-truncates** to `Canaan CanMV-K230 wit…`. Correct behaviour
   rather than overflow, and the identifying half is visible, but the device
   font is wider than the simulator estimate suggested.
4. **The `Radio` row keeps `EU868 · mock` while its chip reads `--`.** Ruled
   deliberate and kept. Region and backend are configuration fetched once, and
   radiod is still configured that way when it is not running, so only the live
   half becomes unknown. The detail is now rendered in the muted treatment
   while the state is unknown, so it stays readable without being mistaken for
   live state, and the row is never blanked. Re-checking this on unit A is what
   turned up the fifth observation below, which is the more serious one.
5. **The `Radio` chip never changed colour. FIXED.** Only its text was
   repainted, so it read `RX` in the same treatment it wore while radiod was
   gone. Found by re-checking observation 4 and recorded in full below.

## The pre-release fix, and the defect the re-check found

Both observations above were fixed and taken back to unit A on the same bench,
build `ab65d03` and then the chip fix on top. The dialog half went as intended;
the radio half did not, and that is the useful part of this section.

**The dialog hierarchy, re-verified at the panel.** `Cancel` now carries the
accent on the power-off dialog and `Power off` takes the restrained treatment;
the warning still reads in full to "then reconnect."; cancelling still reaches
nothing, with `accepted, acting` still at 0 for the boot. The restart dialog is
untouched - `Restart` still blue, same title and body. Four operator
observations, all as designed.

**The radio row was reported fixed, then corrected.** Asked whether the detail
came back to normal after `S60radiod start`, the operator first said it had,
then corrected themselves within the minute: `RX` was back but `EU868 · mock`
was **still dim**. The correction is what stands, and it was right to make -
the first answer would have shipped this.

The muted detail was not the defect. Reproduced in the simulator against the
same pinned LVGL the device runs (`59dc7e4`, shared by the sysroot package and
`vendor/lvgl`), driving a real `radiod --backend mock` through the same
stop-then-start the panel had done, with the resolved style read out of LVGL
rather than judged by eye:

| Radio row | radiod gone | radiod back |
| --- | --- | --- |
| detail text colour | `#5e6670` (muted) | `#8d99a6` (caption) |
| chip foreground | `#8d99a6` | `#8d99a6` |
| chip background | `#10151b` | `#10151b` |

The detail un-mutes correctly, and always did. **The chip never changed at
all.** It was built once in a fixed style and only its *text* was repainted, so
a recovered radio read `RX` in exactly the grey it had worn while radiod was
gone. Nothing on the row brightened on recovery, which is precisely what the
operator saw and reported.

That the eye could not settle it is the point: `#8d99a6` is the caption colour,
already dim beside the near-white row labels, and `#5e6670` is dimmer still.
Asked "is it dim?" with no side-by-side reference, either answer is defensible.
The measurement is not.

Fixed by giving the chip the same treatment the service chips two rows above
have always had - remove the four chip styles, add the one the state calls for
- and by making *which* treatment a decision in `system_view_radio_chip_state()`
so it is covered by the host suite rather than living in an LVGL callback. The
same measurement after the fix:

| Radio row | radiod gone | radiod back |
| --- | --- | --- |
| chip foreground | `#5e6670` | `#06080b` |
| chip background | `#10151b` | `#8ccfff` |

A dim grey chip against a filled ice-blue one, which no one has to squint at.

Two things worth keeping from this. The pure-C view tests could not have caught
it: they assert `radio_state_known` flips, and it always did - the gap was
between the view and the pixels, which is exactly the seam that module split
creates. And a service chip and a radio chip sitting two rows apart behaved
differently for no reason anybody chose; the fix was to make the newer one obey
the older one's rule.

## Not covered here

Wi-Fi association (both wlan interfaces are down on this bench), a board with
a `/data` partition, a battery-bearing base board, and any theme other than
the default. The screen renders `power.source external` with no supplies
because unit A's main board has no gauge.
